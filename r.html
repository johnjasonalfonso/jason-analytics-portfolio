Food Bacteria Statistical Analysis

Tools: R, dplyr, ggplot2, descriptive statistics, and non-parametric hypothesis testing.

This analysis examines bacterial counts, measured as colony-forming units per gram (CFU/g), in chilled and non-chilled food samples. The objective was to describe the observed distributions and investigate whether bacterial counts differed by treatment and observed temperature category.

Research Questions

  • How do observed bacterial counts differ between chilled and non-chilled samples?
  • Are CFU/g distributions consistent with normality?
  • Do observed counts differ across temperature categories?

Sample Overview

The supplied analysis included 96 food samples.

Measure Chilled Non-Chilled
Number of samples 35 61
Mean CFU/g 966 173,137
Median CFU/g 5 98
Maximum CFU/g 19,000 8,000,000

In the supplied results, non-chilled samples had a higher mean and median CFU/g and a wider observed range. Both distributions were strongly right-skewed, indicating that a small number of high values affected the distributions.

Statistical Methods

  • Descriptive statistics: sample size, mean, standard deviation, median, interquartile range, minimum, maximum, skewness, and kurtosis.
  • Shapiro-Wilk tests: assessed the normality of CFU/g values within treatment groups.
  • Wilcoxon rank-sum test: compared observed CFU/g distributions between chilled and non-chilled samples.
  • Kruskal-Wallis and post-hoc tests: examined differences among temperature categories.

Reported Results

Analysis Reported p-value Interpretation
Shapiro-Wilk: chilled 1.47 × 10-13 Evidence against normality
Shapiro-Wilk: non-chilled 2.20 × 10-16 Evidence against normality
Wilcoxon rank-sum 3.00 × 10-6 Reported difference between treatment groups
Kruskal-Wallis: temperature groups 0.0031 Reported difference among temperature categories

The supplied report indicates that observed CFU/g values were generally higher in the non-chilled group and that temperature categories differed in the reported combined analysis. These findings describe the observed sample and do not establish causation or representativeness of the wider food supply.

Limitations and Interpretation

  • The sample may not represent the wider food population, and sample sizes across temperature categories were uneven.
  • The supplied report notes an imputed temperature value for some chilled vegetables. This decision should be checked against the final analysis dataset.
  • Temperature and treatment group are not independent concepts in this dataset, so observed differences should not be interpreted as a causal temperature effect.
  • The reported statistics should be regenerated from the analysis script and checked before being treated as final.

Dengue and Climate Statistical Research

This research collaboration examined relationships between dengue incidence and climate variables using statistical and spatio-temporal analysis.

The analytical work included exploratory data analysis, correlation analysis, trend assessment, and visualization of climate and dengue patterns. The broader research investigated how temperature, rainfall, humidity, and heat index may relate to dengue incidence over time.

Additional charts, methods, and findings will be added as the corresponding analysis files and outputs are prepared for publication.
